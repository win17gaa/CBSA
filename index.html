CHAPTER 1 — LOAD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Load is the total cognitive weight a person carries at any moment. It is the sum
of all active demands on attention, memory, emotional regulation, decision-
making, and environmental processing. Load determines how much capacity remains
for stable behavior.

Load is not a feeling. Load is not stress. Load is not emotion. Load is a
mechanical measurement of how much cognitive bandwidth is consumed.

1.1 — THE THREE COMPONENTS OF LOAD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Load has three parts:

1. WORKING LOAD
The tasks, thoughts, and actions currently being processed. Examples include
driving, talking, planning, navigating, remembering, and monitoring surroundings.
Working load changes second-to-second.

2. RESIDUAL LOAD
The leftover cognitive weight from previous tasks, events, or emotional states.
Examples include unresolved arguments, unfinished tasks, lingering anxiety,
mental clutter, and background worries. Residual load persists even when nothing
is happening.

3. ENVIRONMENTAL LOAD
The cognitive weight imposed by surroundings. Examples include noise, crowds,
movement, unpredictability, clutter, and social complexity. Environmental load is
external but still consumes bandwidth.

1.2 — LOAD IS ALWAYS ADDITIVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Load does not prioritize or negotiate. It simply adds. If working load is 40%,
residual load is 30%, and environmental load is 20%, total load is 90%. At 90%
load, collapse becomes likely. At 100% load, collapse becomes inevitable.

1.3 — LOAD DETERMINES STABILITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Stability is not personality, emotional strength, or discipline. Stability is the
remaining cognitive bandwidth after load is accounted for. High load means low
stability. Low load means high stability.

1.4 — LOAD IS MEASURED BY BEHAVIOR, NOT EMOTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
People often misinterpret load as emotion: overwhelmed, stressed, tired,
irritated. These are symptoms, not measurements. Load is measured by reaction
time, decision quality, error rate, rigidity, impulsivity, avoidance, and
collapse events. Behavior reveals load more accurately than feelings.

1.5 — LOAD IS THE FIRST STABILITY GATE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
If load is too high, nothing else works. Containment fails. Relevance fails.
Telemetry fails. Application fails. Alignment fails. Profile output becomes
distorted. Load is the first gate. If load is not controlled, the architecture
cannot stabilize.

1.6 — LOAD MUST BE REDUCED BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Attempts to fix motivation, discipline, habits, emotions, relationships, or
productivity fail when load is high. Load reduction is the first intervention.
Only after load decreases can the rest of CBSA operate correctly.

1.7 — LOAD IS THE FOUNDATION OF COLLAPSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Collapse is not random, emotional, or dramatic. Collapse is simply load
exceeding capacity. High load leads to low stability, which leads to collapse.
Chapter 2 explains collapse in detail.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
