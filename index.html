How to Use This Tool

1. Log at Your Pace: Submit records when there is data to capture. 7 logs within a 14-day tracking window keep your metrics active.

2. Export Report: Regularly download your local JSON backups.

3. AI Audit: Drop exported files into an AI chat for deep pattern checks.

Core Practice: Pacing & Latches

Watch Your Pace: If your internal pace runs faster than normal, briefly slow down on purpose. Feel the drop, then keep going.

Catch the Latch: Notice what background thoughts you consciously latch onto during the day. If you don't remember them for the night log, they weren't important enough to track.

Informational choices reference list for load reduction:

  • Financial // Actionable Step: Reduce worry loops by shifting from open-ended anxiety to a single concrete action, such as checking due dates or setting a reminder to restore clarity.
  • Financial // Micro Budgeting: Break financial tasks into small actions to prevent overload, such as reviewing one specific account balance instead of all accounts at once.
  • Financial // Decision Pacing: Pause spending decisions when rushed to slow the cognitive loop, such as waiting two minutes before buying something under pressure.
  • Relational // Pacing Pauses: Use a brief pause before responding to prevent drift and misinterpretation, such as waiting two seconds before replying to a confusing statement.
  • Relational // Clarifying Intent: Ask direct questions to reduce relational load and avoid unnecessary conflict, such as saying "What did you mean by that?" instead of assuming negative intent.
  • Relational // Emotional De-escalation: Focus on one relational issue at a time to reduce emotional multitasking, such as addressing a single topic in a conversation instead of multiple grievances at once.
  • Environmental // Sensory Reduction: Lower noise or visual clutter to decrease load and improve clarity, such as turning off background media or moving to a quieter room before deciding.
  • Environmental // Workspace Simplification: Remove small sources of clutter to reduce cognitive friction, such as clearing a single small area of a desk instead of an entire room.
  • Environmental // Predictable Anchors: Maintain fixed physical placements to stabilize pacing, such as keeping keys, wallet, and phone in the exact same location daily.
  • Cognitive // Rush Interrupt: Slow the cognitive loop for two to three seconds when feeling hurried to allow the system to auto-correct, such as pausing briefly before leaving the house to check for essentials.
  • Cognitive // Multitasking Limitation: Focus on one action at a time to preserve working memory, such as finishing a text message before initiating another task.
  • Cognitive // Micro Breaks: Take short pauses to reduce drift and restore baseline regulation, such as looking away from a screen for ten seconds during high-load periods.
  • Emotional // Naming the Emotion: Identify feelings directly to reduce internal friction and prevent drift, such as stating "I feel overwhelmed" out loud.
  • Emotional // Emotional Stacking Prevention: Address one feeling at a time rather than compounding them, such as resolving frustration before tackling underlying worry.
  • Emotional // Grounding Actions: Use simple physical movements to reset internal pacing, such as standing up, stretching, or taking a slow breath.
  • Decision // Decision Pacing: Slow down choice generation because rushing increases load, such as pausing for two seconds before making a selection under time pressure.
  • Decision // Option Reduction: Limit available choices to reduce cognitive friction, such as choosing between two alternatives instead of five.
  • Decision // Relevance Resets: Restate core priorities to prevent distortion, such as asking "What is the main goal here?" before committing to a path.
